Master's thesis

From Text to Returns: Financial News Embeddings as Predictors of Short-Term Abnormal Stock Price Movements

Imperial College London, UK · Jun 2026 - Sep 2026

Supervisors: Dr Arman Khaledian (CEO, Zanista AI) and Dr Philipp Jettkant (Imperial College London)

Completed as part of my MSc in Mathematics and Finance at Imperial College London, in collaboration with Zanista AI, a London-based startup. The thesis uses financial news articles scraped by NewsWitch, a product of Zanista AI, to test whether language model embeddings of news can predict the size of short-term abnormal stock price movements, and how much of that predictability comes from firm-specific information.

Read the thesis (PDF)
Abstract

Large language model (LLM) embeddings provide a numerical representation of financial news that can be used to study its relationship with stock-price movements. This thesis uses LLM embeddings of financial news to investigate whether the information contained in news articles can predict the magnitude of subsequent short-term abnormal price movements, and examines the role of firm-specific information in this relationship. Using news articles covering 14 large-cap U.S. stocks from January 2024 to July 2025, the study compares the predictive performance of original “raw news” embeddings with residualised “pure news” embeddings, from which information associated with observable firm characteristics has been removed. Predictive performance is evaluated across 1-day, 5-day, and 21-day forecasting horizons using absolute abnormal returns as the measure of stock-price movement. At the 1-day and 5-day horizons, “raw news” consistently outperforms “pure news”, while predictive performance is substantially weaker for both representations at the 21-day horizon, where “pure news” performs better in relative terms. Further analysis shows a positive association between the amount of firm-specific information encoded in an embedding and its predictive performance at the shorter horizons. Moreover, reintroducing firm identity into the “pure news” representation largely restores the predictive performance of “raw news” at the 1-day and 5-day horizons. These findings suggest that firm-specific information is an important component of the information in financial news that is useful for identifying the magnitude of short-term abnormal stock-price movements.

Academic projects

Projects from my coursework. Each one links to the full report.

Extreme Value-Based Tail Risk Modelling for Financial Returns

Course: Quantitative Risk Management

Builds a parametric framework for forecasting the tail risk of Tesla (TSLA) stock returns. A GARCH(1,1) volatility model is combined with normal, Student-t and Generalised Pareto (Extreme Value Theory) assumptions for the return shocks to produce Value-at-Risk and Expected Shortfall forecasts, which are then backtested with Kupiec's coverage test. The comparison shows that the choice of distribution matters most at extreme confidence levels, where the heavy-tailed models give more reliable coverage.

Reinforcement Learning Based Optimal Trade Execution

Course: Reinforcement Learning

Re-implements an optimal trade execution framework that uses reinforcement learning to adapt the Almgren-Chriss schedule, tested on AAPL order book data. Tabular Q-learning, SARSA, DDQN and PPO are compared against the Almgren-Chriss benchmark in a single simulator. The results show that the apparent ranking of the algorithms depends heavily on design choices such as time conditioning and how results are aggregated.

Numerical Pricing of Options: Lattice and Finite Difference Methods

Course: Numerical Methods in Finance

Implements numerical methods for pricing options where no closed-form formula exists. Trinomial trees are used for European, American and path-dependent lookback options, and finite difference schemes (explicit, implicit and Crank-Nicolson) price options under a local volatility model that produces a volatility skew. The same tools value down-and-in barrier options, showing how local volatility raises prices when the barrier is low.

Volatility Modelling and Risk Forecasting for the EURO STOXX 50

Course: Quantitative Risk Management

Studies the return behaviour of the EURO STOXX 50 index and builds volatility models for it, from GARCH to ARMA-GARCH with Student-t shocks. The models are then used to forecast one-day Value-at-Risk and Expected Shortfall with historical simulation and filtered historical simulation, and the forecasts are backtested and compared with the Diebold-Mariano test. Filtered historical simulation with GARCH volatility gave the most accurate forecasts, while the EWMA version is the simpler alternative.

Download the report (PDF)

Critical Replication of an SVD and PCA Study of Global Equity Indices

Course: Statistical Methods in Finance

Critically examines a published study that uses Singular Value Decomposition and Principal Component Analysis to reconstruct some stock indices from others. After replicating the original results, the project shows that the apparent accuracy comes from how the data was split and processed, and links the unstable cases to the conditioning of the underlying linear systems. The findings show that PCA describes shared movement across markets well but cannot be relied on for forecasting.
